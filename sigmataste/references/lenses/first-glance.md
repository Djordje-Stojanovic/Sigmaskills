# First glance

## What it sees

Where the eye lands first, and whether it lands anywhere. Look for one second, look away, and name one thing: a subject, a word, a number, a face. "A website" or "a dashboard" is no answer. Then look for five seconds as a stranger: what is it, why stay, what to do next? Then squint until the detail blurs: the frame should fall into two or three masses of clearly different size. Many small masses of the same size mean everything is medium and nothing leads.

Works on any medium: a page's first screen, a dashboard's top row, a slide, a thumbnail at feed size, a film's first frame and hook, a terminal screen.

## Tells

- Three or more elements of equal weight competing (headline, image and button the same visual size).
- The most important number or action sits below the fold or in body text.
- A hero that could be any product: big heading, small grey subline, two buttons, stock image.
- The first video frame is black, a logo, or mid-sentence.
- A dashboard opens on a filter bar and settings, with the answer three rows down.

## Fix moves

- Pick the one thing. Make it decisively bigger, brighter or more isolated than everything else: one size step is not enough; jump two.
- Demote the rest: smaller, quieter colour, more space around the lead element.
- Move the answer to where the eye lands (top left in left-to-right reading, centre on a thumbnail, first second of a video).
- Cut one element from the first screen.

## Verify

- Before and after screenshot at the real viewing size; show each to someone (or a fresh agent) for one second and ask what they saw.
- Squint test, or a blurred screenshot: count the masses.

# Only this one

## What it sees

Whether the look and words come from this subject, or from the category. Cover the logo and swap the name for a rival's: does every screen, slide or frame still work unchanged? Then it was a default, not a decision. Name the one thing only this target has: its object, place, material, number, people, history, sound. Check that the design is built from it. "Clean, modern, premium" is not an idea.

After the first screen or minute: does the idea carry on, or turn into stock blocks?

## Tells

- The swap test passes for a rival with only the logo changed.
- Stock photos, generic 3D blobs, abstract gradients or icon rows where the real product, data or place could be.
- A tagline or headline a competitor could sign ("The platform for modern teams").
- The idea shows on the first screen and then disappears into a generic template.
- A deck theme picked from a preset list, unrelated to the talk.

## Fix moves

- Write a one-line point of view from the subject's world, and give it a two-word name ("Field Notebook", "Control Tower", "Paper Ledger"). Every later choice answers to it.
- Replace one generic visual with the real thing: a screenshot, the actual data, the product, the place, the people.
- Borrow vocabulary from the subject: materials, instruments, signage, documents of that world.
- Carry the idea through: one recurring device that returns in every section, slide or act.

## Verify

- The swap test on the after version: swap the name for a rival's and the design should feel wrong.
- Ask a stranger what kind of thing it is from the visuals alone, with the name covered.

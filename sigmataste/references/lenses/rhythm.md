# Rhythm

## What it sees

The shape of the whole, section after section. Give each section, slide or scene one word for its layout: split, list, grid, full bleed, statement, table, quote, card. The same word three times in a row is the template showing. Then ask whether each block has the right form: prose carries an argument, a list carries parallel items, a table carries the same fields repeated. And whether density fits the job: a speaker deck wants one idea per slide; a reading deck or report wants self-contained detail.

## Tells

- Title, three bullets, image: on every slide.
- Every page section the same centred heading plus a card grid.
- A film where every scene is footage plus a lower third.
- Bullet lists where two sentences would read better; a table for two items; prose for a list of eight parallel facts.
- Text shrunk to fit instead of split into two slides or sections.
- A slide with little content and tiny text in a big empty field.

## Fix moves

- Write the layout word per section or slide. Break any run of three: a statement slide, a full-bleed image, a table, a quote.
- Pick the form per block on purpose (prose, list, table, callout, code) and say why in the plan.
- Set density from the use: speaker-led (one idea, big type, more slides) or reading-first (more detail per slide, strong structure). Do not mix inside one deck.
- When content overflows, split; when it is thin, make it bigger.

## Verify

- The list of layout words before and after: no run of three of the same.
- A contact sheet or thumbnail grid of all slides, sections or scenes: the after has visible variety and one family look.

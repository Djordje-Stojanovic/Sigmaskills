# The pitch

## What it sees

The argument of anything that must persuade: a landing page, a pricing page, a pitch deck, a store listing, a thumbnail and title, a video hook. In five seconds a stranger should know what it is, why it matters to them, and the one thing to do. Then the page should answer "so what?" and "prove it" before it asks again.

## Tells

- A headline that says what the product is made of, not what changes for the reader.
- Two or more primary actions of equal weight.
- Claims with no proof near them ("Trusted by thousands", "industry-leading").
- Features listed without the consequence for the reader.
- Objections left unanswered (price, "will it work for me?", effort to switch, risk).
- The hook or title promises one thing and the first screen shows another.
- A strong middle and a flat end: no last action, no strong final moment.

## Fix moves

- Rewrite the headline as the reader's outcome, specific and checkable. Keep the target's facts; mark gaps `[NEED: proof]`.
- One primary action per screen; secondary actions visibly quieter.
- Put proof next to the claim it supports: a number, a named customer, a real screenshot, a review.
- Add the "which means..." bridge from each feature to the reader's gain.
- Answer the top two objections where they arise (near price, near the action).
- Design the end on purpose: the last screen, slide or second carries the action and one strong moment.

## Verify

- Five-second test with a stranger (or a fresh agent shown the screenshot): what is it, who is it for, what do I do?
- Every claim in the after version has proof next to it or a `[NEED]` mark.

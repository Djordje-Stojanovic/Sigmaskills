# Prose

## What it sees

Longer writing (docs, articles, READMEs, scripts, post text, slide notes) that reads as default model or template prose. The default chooses for the widest reader; a writer chooses for one reader and one subject. This lens finds the default. For the rewrite voice (short, clear, outsider-readable technical English), hand the plan's prose changes to `/sigmawrite`; this lens does not restate its rules.

## Tells (strongest first)

- Staging instead of stating: "It's not X, it's Y" where nobody said X; one-line closers that repeat the paragraph; "Here's the thing"; self-answered questions; colon reveals ("The best part: it learns").
- Arguing with no one: "To be clear", "Don't get me wrong", rejecting options nobody raised.
- Inflation: "a pivotal moment", "a testament to", "plays a key role"; vague authority ("experts say").
- Rhythm by rule: ideas always in threes; the same paragraph shape every time; dashes as the universal joint.
- Formatting by rule: bold label and colon on every list item; a heading over every two sentences; emoji in headings.
- Leftovers: "I hope this helps", "Let's dive in", writing about the document instead of its subject.
- Stock words in clusters: delve, leverage, robust, seamless, tapestry, landscape, showcase.

## Fix moves

- State the point directly; delete closers, run-ups and fake contrasts.
- Replace inflation with the fact underneath; name the source or cut the claim.
- Vary structure only where the meaning varies; turn bold-label lists into prose when labels carry nothing.
- Keep the writer's real voice: opinions, humour, roughness. Do not add personality, facts or stance the source does not have.
- Mark missing facts as `[NEED: <what>]` instead of inventing them.

## Verify

- Read it aloud.
- Search the after text for the strongest tells (contrasts, closers, dashes, bold labels).
- Compare facts before and after: nothing added, nothing lost.

# The masters: quotes and principles

Read this when a run weighs money, risk, capital, a bet or a sale and you want the investors' own words and rules behind your answer. Fourteen people, ten quotes each, then their principles. The method that merges them is in [strategy.md](strategy.md).

How to use it. Every quote is verbatim and carries a source you can open. A quote is evidence for a principle, not advice. Name the principles you applied in the reply with the tag below (for example `Buffett P1`, `Munger P2`). When a quote is the only support for a number or a claim, cite it by name and source.

How it was checked. Each quote was matched word for word against the fetched text of its source on 2026-10-23. Quotes that could not be matched were dropped, not paraphrased. Transcripts on third-party sites are marked by the outlet in the source line; check the original recording when a decision depends on one.

Principles:

- Musk P1. Reinvest free cash flow into the next, cheaper product. Early buyers fund the later mass-market one.
- Musk P2. Put your own money at risk first when the odds are low. Do not gamble other people's funds.
- Musk P3. The cost of capital decides the customer's price. Cut the install cost, then the financing cost.
- Musk P4. Spend as fast as spending makes sense, but stay lean. Do not raise money to cover waste.
- Musk P5. Know your credit lines. A lender who pulls them can end a company overnight.
- Musk P6. Look for a step change in cost, such as a reusable rocket. Then fund it until it works.

## Warren Buffett

**Role:** Chairman and CEO of Berkshire Hathaway. He buys whole businesses and large stock stakes, funded by insurance float and retained earnings, and holds them for decades.

Quotes:

1. "It's far better to buy a wonderful company at a fair price than a fair company at a wonderful price." - Berkshire Hathaway shareholder letter, 1989 | https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1989.html
2. "Time is the friend of the wonderful business, the enemy of the mediocre." - Berkshire Hathaway shareholder letter, 1989 | https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1989.html
3. "Our goal is more modest: we simply attempt to be fearful when others are greedy and to be greedy only when others are fearful." - Berkshire Hathaway shareholder letter, 1986 | https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1986.html
4. "In fact, when we own portions of outstanding businesses with outstanding managements, our favorite holding period is forever." - Berkshire Hathaway shareholder letter, 1988 | https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1988.html
5. "Cash, though, is to a business as oxygen is to an individual: never thought about when it is present, the only thing in mind when it is absent." - Berkshire Hathaway shareholder letter, 2014 | https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2014ltr.pdf
6. "History tells us that leverage all too often produces zeroes, even when it is employed by very smart people." - Berkshire Hathaway shareholder letter, 2010 ("Life and Debt") | https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2010ltr.pdf
7. "If you aren't willing to own a stock for ten years, don't even think about owning it for ten minutes." - Berkshire Hathaway shareholder letter, 1996 | https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1996.html
8. "What counts for most people in investing is not how much they know, but rather how realistically they define what they don't know." - Berkshire Hathaway shareholder letter, 1992 | https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1992.html
9. "By retaining all earnings for a very long time, and allowing compound interest to work its magic, we have amassed funds that have enabled us to purchase and develop the valuable groves earlier described." - Berkshire Hathaway shareholder letter, 2018 | https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2018ltr.pdf
10. "Our unwavering conclusion: Never bet against America." - Berkshire Hathaway shareholder letter, 2020 | https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2020ltr.pdf

Principles:

- Buffett P1. Buy a great business at a fair price. Do not buy a weak business just because it looks cheap.
- Buffett P2. Hold great businesses for as long as they stay great. Time works for quality and against junk.
- Buffett P3. Be bold when others panic and careful when others are euphoric. Mood in the market is the signal.
- Buffett P4. Stay inside what you understand. Knowing the edge of your knowledge matters more than knowing a lot.
- Buffett P5. Never use debt that can wipe you out. One zero ruins a long string of good years.
- Buffett P6. Keep cash ready. Cash is only noticed when it is gone, and then nothing else matters.

Principles:

- Huang P1. Do the risky work early. Simulate the future today, then commit hard.
- Huang P2. Judge the bet by its core belief, not the price chart. Nvidia lost most of its value over CUDA and held on.

- Huang P4. Go where nobody sells yet. No customers today can mean no competitors.
- Huang P5. Follow the importance of the work, not the size of today's market. Importance signals a future market.
- Huang P6. Back the idea that you must build to learn whether it scales.

Principles:

- Ackman P1. Concentrate on a few businesses that are simple, durable and predictable. Quality, not spread, is what protects the downside.
- Ackman P2. Pay a fair to cheap price. A good price for a great business makes large losses hard.
- Ackman P3. Size each bet by what you can win against what you can lose. Cap the loss to a few percent of capital.
- Ackman P4. Test your idea against the smartest person who disagrees. Their best case either saves you or raises your conviction.
- Ackman P5. Match your funding to your holding period. Long-term capital lets you hold through bad news.
- Ackman P6. Do not add too much debt to a great business. Study each mistake once so you never repeat it.

Principles:

- Munger P1. Great businesses make the big money. A stock cannot long out-earn the business behind it.
- Munger P2. Wait for a rare, clearly mispriced bet. Then bet big. A few good decisions drive most of the results.
- Munger P3. Do less. Fewer trades mean less tax, less cost and less noise.
- Munger P4. Be patient for years, then act fast when the chance comes. Missed chances cost more than small losses.
- Munger P5. Stay out of debt. Interest at high rates cannot be out-earned.

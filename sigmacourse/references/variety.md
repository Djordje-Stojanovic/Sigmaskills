# Variety

Variety exists so the learner can use an ability on material they have not seen. Draw it from the map, never from a template: the number of shapes, task types and media follows the number of lessons and what each proof needs. Every list below is examples; the grill drops, renames and adds in the field's own words.

Each lesson declares four fields in its front matter, and a script checks the mix:

- `shape:` one lesson shape;
- `tasks:` one or more task types;
- `level:` the hardest item in it, on the field's ladder;
- `media:` the media pieces it carries, each with a spec id.

Draw at random from the shapes that can carry the node's proof task, seeded by the lesson id, so a rerun gives the same draw. The writer may override a draw that does not fit and logs why (`mix_override: <reason>`). The page frame stays the same (where the video sits, where the task sits, where the answer opens); the content does not.

## Shapes

Work first, then teach · Guess, then reveal · Hunt for something hard to find in real material · Error hunt in real work · Guided build with checkpoints · Derivation with a problem set · Timed drill · Read a real document, then decide by a deadline · Case discussion · Compare a success and a failure from the same start · Book chapter, then a task · Video that pauses and asks · Long lecture · Documentary · Teacher debate, the learner writing their own rule · Interview a teacher · Oral defence · Role-play a real situation · Explorable (move a control, see the effect) · Branching scenario · Quick mixed quiz · Write or teach back · Field task revisited later · Explain, example, practice (for fast basics).

## Task types

In your head · pen and paper · with the field's tool · recall from memory · predict before the reveal · estimate · spot the error · rank or sort · build from blank · write · speak · perform or demonstrate · teach back · timed drill · open-book research · capstone · pick the best of several strong candidates · decide with a deadline.

## Levels

An ordered ladder in the field's words, its top set by the end lines. One example: recognise · recall · explain in your words · apply to a worked example · do it on real material · handle a real case with no clean answer · the field's hardest exam · working level · expert level · live, under real stakes. Each part sits higher than the parts it needs.

## Sameness checks (script, per unit of progress the spec names)

1. No two lessons in a row share a shape, when the unit has more than one shape that fits.
2. No fixed opener or closer: flag the same first-sentence pattern, heading sequence or closing line in consecutive lessons.
3. Outcomes and answers stay hidden until the learner commits, in no fixed order, and some surprise (the obvious answer was wrong).
4. Old abilities come back in quizzes, tasks and cumulative tests, as the spacing plan in SKILL.md step 4 lists.
5. A lesson fits the learner's real sitting length; longer work splits into sittings.
6. The script prints the share of learner time spent doing (tasks, practice, proofs) against taking in (reading, watching, listening); the grill sets the floor.
7. Repetition by content, not wrapping: one topic in many formats is still one topic. Count repeated titles and repeated figures.

The reviewer, not a script, judges: does the lesson teach one thing well, and would the learner know what to do at once?

## Case files

When the field has cases (a past decision, a patient, a match to analyse, a bug), spread them on axes a schema check counts, chosen in the grill; for example kind, outcome, how much help, length, sub-field, place and time. Show a curated case beside how often its kind turns out that way. Hold some cases back, unseen, for the proofs.

## Media

A piece of media earns its place by teaching what text cannot: a demonstration, a real moment on tape, a mechanism in motion, a voice. One that reads the text aloud fails review. Real footage beats a generated scene when it shows the same point. Every piece ends in active work. Kinds to draw from: a film where a whole module needs a story, a lesson video, a slice of real footage, a long lecture, a short explainer, an animation from calculated data, a 3D shot where depth teaches, an audio version, diagrams and charts. Generated images serve atmosphere only, never data.

When the user names a lecture they love, fetch it, check its title, date and length, and write it as the rubric for long lectures. Estimate render hours from measured speeds before an unattended run.
